# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary users are elementary and junior-high-school teachers working in multilingual Philippine
classrooms. They use SalinAral while preparing an existing lesson for learners who may understand
difficult concepts more readily through a Philippine or regional language.

Secondary users may include Alternative Learning System educators, tutors, local education programs,
Indigenous Peoples Education facilitators, and parents performing the same lesson preparation job.
SalinAral remains teacher-facing and is not a student platform.

## Product Purpose

SalinAral is an accountless, stateless lesson-localisation workspace. It helps a teacher turn
existing English or Filipino material into an editable, grade-appropriate regional-language lesson
draft containing a faithful translation, a simplified explanation, a glossary, and short
comprehension questions.

Success means reducing repetitive preparation work while preserving the teacher's control over the
lesson. Every generated result is an **AI-assisted draft—review before classroom use**. The product
provides auxiliary linguistic scaffolding; it does not replace the teacher or prescribe a medium of
instruction.

## Positioning

SalinAral provides teacher-controlled lesson localisation: it creates a reviewable regional-language
draft while the teacher retains authority over every correction and classroom decision.

## Brand Personality

SalinAral is calm, capable, and humane. Its voice is clear and respectful, with the confidence of a
dependable working tool rather than the personality of an automated expert. It supports teacher
judgment, explains consequential states plainly, and avoids promotional claims about AI.

## Anti-references

SalinAral must not resemble an AI chatbot. Avoid prompt-first layouts, conversational assistant
framing, glowing AI motifs, and copy that gives the system authority over the teacher. It must also
avoid decorative interfaces that turn lesson preparation into a performance rather than a focused
editing task.

## Design Principles

1. **Teacher authority is visible.** Make source material, generated text, warnings, and editing
   controls easy to inspect; never imply that an unreviewed draft is verified.
2. **The lesson comes first.** Keep the interface quiet enough for reading, comparison, correction,
   and reuse to remain the visual focus.
3. **Value begins immediately.** Do not place registration, provider selection, a tutorial, or
   complex setup before the first useful action.
4. **Review is part of the workflow.** Treat OCR correction, preservation warnings, and the
   AI-assisted draft label as functional steps rather than disclaimers hidden at the edge.
5. **Privacy is legible.** Reinforce stateless processing without exposing implementation jargon or
   suggesting that lesson content is saved.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA and strengthen the baseline for classroom conditions: high text contrast,
readable type, visible focus, complete keyboard operation, large touch targets, and phone-sized
layouts. Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences and never carry meaning by itself. The
workspace should remain usable on constrained devices and low-bandwidth connections. Status,
warnings, and language maturity must not rely on color alone.
